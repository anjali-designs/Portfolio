Novel Office website

I restructured and redesigned the website of Novel Office, a managed workspace provider in Bengaluru, so people can find a location, see pricing and enquire without getting lost.

Role
Product designer: research, IA, UI, responsive design, dev handoff
Scope
Homepage, 5 solution pages, 7 location pages, property pages, about, careers
Status
Live at noveloffice.in

The problem

People came to the site to check location, pricing, facilities and what spaces were available. They had to dig through several pages to reach a location, couldn’t find pricing, and faced long enquiry forms.

“Sometimes there was too much information, and I wasn’t sure what I should do next. I’d rather just speak to someone.” An ops manager, in an interview

Research

A survey of 20 people who had searched for office space. What came up:

  • It wasn’t clear what Novel Office offers: services were hard to find and there was little information about what the company does.
  • Pricing wasn’t shown, so people had to contact someone just to find out.
  • Many browse on mobile and prefer a quick enquiry to a long form.
  • Some had struggled to navigate workspace sites, and rated location and pricing as the information that matters most.

Interviews with HR, admin and operations managers who book office space. Two things they said:

“I could find it, but I had to go through a few pages before getting to the specific location.”

“I’d prefer a quick enquiry or callback instead of filling out a long form.”

Competitors: WeWork, Awfis, Smartworks and Bangalore Office. They do clear visuals and structured flows well, but hide pricing and lean on long enquiry forms. That gap became our direction.

Key decisions

  • Location first. Locations moved into the main navigation, with a listing page that switches between office space and coworking, and a property page for each centre with a “Schedule a visit” button. Answer to: “I had to go through a few pages.”
  • Pricing before the form. Every solution page shows 3 pricing cards before asking for contact details. Answer to: pricing wasn’t shown and people couldn’t tell what we offer.
  • A 4-field enquiry. Name, email, phone and location (or office type) in a pop-up, available on every page. Answer to: people preferred a quick enquiry over a long form.
  • Mobile first. Locations, features and testimonials as swipeable cards, built for the many people browsing on phones.
  • Trust where it’s needed. Photo galleries, client logos, testimonials and FAQs sit next to the decision points, because people said good photos and clear details made them trust the site.

Information architecture

Home, Solutions (office space, coworking, meeting rooms, virtual office, business centre), Locations (all locations, property pages), About, Careers and Services. Scroll across, or click to preview.

Design system

Deep teal (#014248) with a teal accent (#39B3BA), Montserrat for display and Poppins for UI, a spacing scale from 4 to 96 px, and button, form, card and icon rules. Defined before the pages so 13+ pages stayed consistent.

Final screens

Home, solution pages, locations, property pages, about and careers. It’s live at noveloffice.in.

What I learned

  • Many people we surveyed couldn’t tell what Novel Office does, so clear structure and services mattered more than visuals.
  • Pricing up front wasn’t the norm among competitors, and the survey made the case for it.
  • Measuring after launch turned the redesign from opinion into evidence.

What’s next

Usability tests on the enquiry and visit booking journeys, and tracking enquiry conversion, not just engagement.
