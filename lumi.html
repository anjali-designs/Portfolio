Lumi

Lumi is Novel Office’s internal AI assistant. It answers the scenario questions training never covers, using only knowledge that people have reviewed and approved.

Role
Product designer: research workshops, IA, flows, UI, design system
Platform
Web app, desktop first
Scope
60+ screens: employee, team lead and admin dashboards, AI chat, knowledge creation and approval
Status
Phase 1 designed and in development, phase 2 planned

The problem

Our LMS covers SOPs, training modules and definitions. It doesn’t cover real client scenarios, past decisions, email threads, exceptions or team learnings. So new joiners keep asking seniors the same questions, and that knowledge leaves when people do. Leadership wanted AI to become the company’s collective memory.

The kind of questions people ask:

“What is rental escalation?”

“The client isn’t accepting a 7% increase. Show me previous cases.”

“Who handles this account?”

What made it hard

  • Sensitive data. HR, payroll, finance and legal knowledge must never reach the wrong person through a chat answer.
  • Wrong answers are worse than no answer. An AI that guesses about a client contract does real damage.
  • Knowledge crosses departments. One email thread can touch sales, operations and finance.
  • Contributing has to be easy, or the knowledge base stays empty.

Key decisions

  • Permissions before the prompt. Lumi only searches knowledge the person’s role and department allow. A software employee’s answers can never draw on HR, payroll, finance or legal. I ran risk-focused workshops to map these rules before designing any screen.
  • “I don’t have enough knowledge” is a real answer. When nothing approved matches, Lumi says so and creates a ticket in an admin queue. The admin writes the answer, assigns a department and publishes it, so the gap is closed for everyone.
  • Contribute, review, publish. Every piece of knowledge goes through its department’s team lead. When it touches more than one department, approvals run in parallel instead of in a chain.
  • Forward an email, get a draft. Lumi extracts the content, drafts an article and detects the department. The contributor only reviews and submits, which makes sharing a quick job.
  • Every status is visible. Draft, pending, in review, revision required, approved, rejected, published and archived, with version history, so nobody wonders where their submission went.

Outcome

Phase 1 is designed, handed off and in development. These are the success metrics we’ll track after launch (planned, not results yet):

  • Share of questions answered from approved knowledge
  • Time from “unanswered” to a published answer
  • Fewer repeat questions to seniors, measured with a short survey before and after

What I learned

  • In an internal AI tool, permissions and approvals are the product, not paperwork around it.
  • Designing the “no answer” state first made the whole system more trustworthy than any clever answer screen.
  • Making contribution effortless (forward an email) mattered more than making the approval screens pretty.

What’s next

Phase 2: richer feedback than thumbs up or down, analytics on where knowledge is thin, and support for images, voice notes and meeting recordings.
