Meeting Room Booking System

While redesigning the Novel Office website, I noticed competitors let people book meeting rooms online while we didn’t, so I made the flows and screens for booking a room online.

Role
Product designer: IA, user flows, UI
Scope
Find, check, book and manage rooms across 7 office locations (1 to 3 rooms each)
Status
Demo phase, built on the existing Novel design system

The problem

Competitors let people book a meeting room online. Novel Office didn’t. Meeting rooms are a side offering for us, so the solution had to be small, cheap to build and easy to run.

Constraints that shaped the design

  • Small inventory: 7 locations with 1 to 3 rooms each. No need for maps or complex search; simple filters do the job.
  • Existing design system: no new components unless unavoidable, so devs could build it fast.
  • Side offering: scope limited to the core job of booking and managing a room.

Key decisions

  • Filters people actually use: capacity, layout and facilities, because that’s how people describe the room they need.
  • The whole day at a glance: availability shown as a day view, so free and booked slots are obvious before choosing.
  • One booking sheet: pick a slot and confirm in one place instead of a multi-step form.
  • Unhappy paths first: clashes, expired holds, cancellations and changes were mapped before the UI, so devs had every state from day one.
  • Manage without calling anyone: change the time, add guests or release a room from “My bookings”.

User flow

From intent to confirmation, including clashes, expired holds and changes. Scroll across, or click to preview.

Final screens

Built on the existing Novel design system, so type, components and interaction states stay consistent with the rest of the product.

Outcome

The flows and screens are done and in the demo phase. Next: usability tests with the people who book rooms most, then tracking bookings completed online versus by phone.

What I learned

  • Spotting a gap while working on something else is part of the job. I wouldn’t have found this if I’d only stayed inside the website brief.
  • A small scope is a design constraint, not a weakness: it pushed me to keep the flow short.
  • Designing errors and cancellations early saved a round of back-and-forth with devs.
